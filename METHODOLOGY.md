# Methodology

Research & Decision Systems publishes source-backed analytical work using explicit evidence classes and revision rules.

## Evidence classes

- **Verified fact:** established by primary records or multiple independent high-quality sources.
- **Supported reporting:** responsibly reported but not independently established by the project.
- **Analytical inference:** a reasoned conclusion derived from documented evidence and explicit assumptions.
- **Forecast:** a probabilistic judgment about a future outcome.
- **Unresolved claim:** material but insufficiently established.
- **Not established:** the available record does not support the proposition.

## Research sequence

1. Define the decision question or historical claim.
2. Establish the baseline and chronology.
3. Locate primary records and identify source provenance.
4. Separate evidence from interpretation.
5. Test the strongest contrary case.
6. Identify missing evidence, constraints, and falsifiers.
7. State confidence and revision conditions.
8. Preserve publication dates and material corrections.

## Publication principles

- Primary records are preferred over repeated summaries.
- Facts, inferences, forecasts, and speculation are labeled separately.
- Historical dossiers are evaluated for provenance, completeness, and evidentiary limits.
- Policy analysis distinguishes legal text, institutional position, political proposal, and operational implementation.
- Interface design must not imply live data, certainty, or functionality that does not exist.

## Revision standard

Published assessments may be corrected, downgraded, expanded, or withdrawn when new primary records, legal findings, official decisions, or material contradictions become available.
