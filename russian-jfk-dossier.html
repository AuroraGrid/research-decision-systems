The Russian JFK Dossier

An analysis of the files delivered to Representative Anna Paulina Luna and what they do—and do not—establish about Lee Harvey Oswald and Soviet knowledge of the Kennedy assassination.

A project by Hasan Kazmi · Research direction and analytical framing by Hasan Kazmi · AI-assisted synthesis produced with Manus AI

Executive judgment

A curated dossier, not the missing KGB file

The material is historically and geopolitically notable, but it does not resolve the central mysteries of the assassination.

Official titleThe Assassination of John F. Kennedy and Soviet-American Relations
Length350 pages
Period covered1959–1964
Document typeCurated Soviet-era digest with modern Russian commentary
Binding limitation: the dossier is not the complete KGB surveillance file on Lee Harvey Oswald. Researchers have described that missing file as far larger and potentially more consequential.

Key findings

What the dossier adds

01

High-level Russian preparation

The dossier includes modern commentary associated with Andrey Artizov, head of Russia's Federal Archival Agency. Its editorial board was chaired by Sergei Naryshkin, director of Russia's Foreign Intelligence Service.

02

The Kennedy–Khrushchev “World Peace Bridge”

A hand-drawn map proposed a bridge across the Bering Strait connecting Alaska and Siberia. The item generated modern geopolitical attention but does not materially clarify the assassination.

03

Known records, newly packaged

Much of the material was already familiar to specialist researchers. The new value lies primarily in curation, presentation, and official Russian framing.

04

Strategic narrative risk

Because the release was curated through senior Russian state and intelligence institutions, it should be read as a constructed disclosure—not as a neutral release of the complete underlying record.
