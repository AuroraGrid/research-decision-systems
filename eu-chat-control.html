EU Chat Control Monitor

A disciplined tracker of the EU's temporary voluntary-scanning derogation and the separate permanent regulation to prevent and combat online child sexual abuse.

Research direction, analysis, and publication by Hasan Kazmi

Executive status

Two legal tracks must not be confused

Temporary measure

Reinstated through April 3, 2028

The earlier derogation expired on April 3, 2026. The European Parliament adopted a position to reinstate it on July 9, and the Council gave final approval on July 23. The measure permits providers to resume voluntary detection, reporting, and removal of child sexual abuse material.

Permanent regulation

Negotiations remain ongoing

The long-term Regulation to Prevent and Combat Child Sexual Abuse remains under the ordinary legislative procedure. The Council and Parliament are still negotiating the permanent framework.

Correction to the April version: the legal gap did not remain open. EU institutions reinstated the temporary derogation in July 2026. The permanent proposal has not been finally adopted.

Legislative timeline

Key institutional actions

  1. April 3, 2026

    Previous temporary derogation expired

    The 2021 interim measure, extended in 2024, lapsed and created a legal gap for voluntary scanning.

  2. June 29, 2026

    Permanent-regulation trilogue continued

    Negotiators met on the long-term CSAR proposal; subsequent Council documents described the outcome and next steps.

  3. July 2, 2026

    Council adopted its position on reinstatement

    The Council sought a new temporary measure running until April 3, 2028.

  4. July 9, 2026

    European Parliament adopted its position

    Parliament approved the reinstatement text with amendments.

  5. July 23, 2026

    Council gave final approval

    The temporary measure was cleared for publication in the Official Journal and entry into force three days later.

What the monitor tracks

Decision-relevant distinctions

Institutional status

Which institution has acted, what legal instrument is involved, and whether the action is a negotiating mandate, provisional agreement, or final adoption.

Technology and rights

How proposals affect encrypted communications, age verification, voluntary detection, risk-mitigation duties, oversight, and redress.

Temporary versus permanent law

The temporary ePrivacy derogation is a bridge measure. The permanent CSAR proposal is a separate legislative file with broader obligations.

Revision triggers

The page is revised when the Official Journal publishes the final temporary regulation or when Parliament and Council reach a verified agreement on the permanent proposal.
